# Health and Safety File sign off: who may sign, and what the engine checks

Prepared 23/09/2026 for the Director, on the instruction that Occupational Medical Practitioners do not sign Health and Safety Files: a competent, health and safety qualified person does.

## 1. How this was researched, and its limits

1.1 Desk research on 23/09/2026. Every source page was blocked by the build environment, so each point below rests on search engine extracts only, not on a direct reading. The sources are listed in section 7.

1.2 Regulation numbers in this note are leads to confirm against the Gazette text of the Construction Regulations, 2014 before the engine or any page relies on them. They are kept out of every public page and every File until then (contract section 7).

1.3 This is not legal advice. The attorney and a registered practitioner should confirm section 5 before release 1.0 of the File engine.

## 2. What the sources say

2.1 **Construction work (Construction Regulations, 2014).**

2.1.1 The health and safety file is a defined record in permanent form. The principal contractor opens and keeps it on site and hands a consolidated file to the client when the work is complete. The client must ensure it is kept, and audits it at regular intervals.

2.1.2 Where a construction work permit is required, the client appoints a competent person in writing as its agent, and that agent must be registered with the statutory body the Chief Inspector has approved.

2.1.3 The principal contractor appoints a full time competent construction manager in writing. A contractor may not appoint a construction health and safety officer unless reasonably satisfied that the officer is registered with the approved body.

2.1.4 The approved body is the South African Council for the Project and Construction Management Professions (SACPCMP). Its construction health and safety categories are Professional Construction Health and Safety Agent (Pr CHSA), Construction Health and Safety Manager (CHSM) and Construction Health and Safety Officer (CHSO), each with a candidate category.

2.1.5 Not confirmed: whether the Regulations themselves make CHSM registration mandatory. Treat it as a client or market requirement until the Gazette text shows otherwise.

2.2 **Other industries.**

2.2.1 No source defines a statutory "Health and Safety File" outside construction. In practice the file evidences the employer's general duty under section 8 of the OHS Act. Claims that a file is "legally required" for every workplace are marketing wording, not a named provision.

2.2.2 The chief executive carries the accountability (section 16(1)) and may assign duties to a person under their control (section 16(2)), while remaining responsible.

2.2.3 A competent person is one with the knowledge, training, experience and, where applicable, qualifications for the task, who is familiar with the Act and Regulations (Construction Regulations definition; the matching definitions elsewhere were not confirmed).

2.2.4 SAIOSH is a professional body recognised by SAQA. Its designations are voluntary, not statutory:
- TechSAIOSH: NQF 5 and two years' experience.
- GradSAIOSH: NQF 6 and three years.
- CMSAIOSH: NQF 7 and five years.

SAMTRAC and the NEBOSH International General Certificate are market practice and training credentials, not registrations.

2.3 **Mining (MHSA).** The employer carries the duty and appoints managers. It may entrust functions to others and notify the Chief Inspector of Mines. No source describes a Health and Safety File under the MHSA; the OHS Act File concept does not apply to mines.

2.4 **Occupational Medical Practitioner.** No source gives the OMP a sign off role over a Health and Safety File. The OMP signs the medical surveillance programme and ratifies surveillance recommendations. Those documents are filed in Section E of the File as evidence; they are not an approval of the File.

## 3. The Director's rule

3.1 A Health and Safety File is signed by a competent, health and safety qualified person, never by an OMP.

3.2 The OMP's signature appears only on the medical surveillance plan and certificates, which the File holds as evidence in Section E.

3.3 The client's section 16(2) appointee accepts the File in writing, and the chief executive acknowledges it. The software and Care Net do not carry the employer's statutory duty.

## 4. Sign off roles in the engine

| Role | Who | Required for release |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safety content sign off | A registered health and safety practitioner whose body and category fit the File type (section 5) | Yes |
| Client acceptance | The client's section 16(2) appointee | Yes |
| Chief executive acknowledgement | The client's section 16(1) chief executive | Recorded, not a gate |
| Medical surveillance evidence | The OMP signed plan, filed in Section E | Evidence item, not a sign off |

## 6. What the engine records and checks for every signatory

6.1 The engine records:
- full name;
- registering body and category or designation;
- registration number;
- registration expiry or renewal date;
- the date the public register of the body was checked, with a reference to the saved proof;
- the written appointment reference;
- the scope of the sign off;
- the decision, the date and the File revision.

6.1.1 Director's instruction (23/09/2026): every signatory must have an **appointment letter** and an **engagement letter** on record before they can sign. Each letter is held as a document reference with its date and, once it exists, the record identifier in Care Net's recruitment portal, so the letters can later be linked from the portal rather than uploaded again. Until the portal exposes an interface, the letters are uploaded and referenced by hand.

6.2 A release is refused when:
- the registration has expired on the decision date;
- the body or category does not fit the File type;
- the register check is missing;
- the appointment letter or the engagement letter is missing;
- the client's section 16(2) acceptance is missing.

6.3 An ID number is not stored. The registration number identifies the practitioner, and POPIA minimality applies.
