## 2. Design principles

2.1 One sign in, two spines. A company contact signs in once and sees both the commercial records Care Net keeps and the medical records MCO keeps, without a second password.

2.2 Care Net holds no clinical detail. The File records that evidence exists, where it lives, who supplied it, its dates and its fingerprint. Certificates of fitness reach the File as outcome, restriction, dates and practitioner identity only (SPEC B10.4). Care Net screens fitness for work and does not diagnose.

2.3 Bytes do not linger. A document passes through Care Net staging on its way to MCO and is deleted from staging once MCO has confirmed it holds an identical copy; bytes that fail the fingerprint check or the security scan, are refused or are never completed are removed too. Before a document can move, its hidden details are removed and the cleaned copy replaces the bytes the company sent (section 4.11). The audit trail keeps the fingerprints and the MCO reference, never the bytes. Documents held while MCO builds the transfer protocol, and documents blocked by a consent withdrawal or a revoked verification, stay in staging, but never for longer than two years (section 4.7), and the company may delete them itself with a PIN at any time before then (section 4.8).

2.4 Consent and verification before storage. Uploads must be open, the company must be verified as a Care Net Consultants client, and three separate, unticked consents (wording version `HSF-CONSENT-1.0`) must be given before the first document is accepted. Withdrawal stops new uploads at once, and withdrawing the storage or the transfer consent also stops the company's documents still in staging from moving (section 4.5).

2.4.1 Scan and clean before movement. Every upload is scanned for viruses, malware and harmful content before it can be held or moved (section 4.6), and all non essential metadata is removed from it (section 4.11). Nothing that has not passed the scan and been cleaned leaves staging for MCO.

2.5 The kernel is the only legal reference. Pages and answers cite an instrument only if it is in `kernel_citable_instrument` (verified three ways, in force, not held). Everything else shows as awaiting verification (contract section 7).
2.5.1 A hold (`msp_instrument_currency_hold`) removes an instrument from citation even while its kernel row still reads verified, and the public register, industry profile and framework statistics views leave it out too (contract 9.4). Holds are seeded for the NIHL Regulations, 2003 and the Environmental Regulations for Workplaces, 1987 wherever the kernel still marks them verified (HSF-7), and for the Asbestos Abatement Regulations, 2020, whose amendment notice number conflicts (GN R.2092 against GN R.11435, register HSF-9).
2.5.2 A File element cites more narrowly (contract 9.3). `hsf_element_citable` is the one definition, used by the public element library, the File detail, the kernel API and the release gate: the instrument must be citable as above, of scope safety or both, and linked to the element with a provision pinned past "awaiting verification". The library seed pins no provision, so until the Phase 2 re verification every File element shows its instruments as "Awaiting verification". That is the truthful state, not a fault.

2.6 Portable by construction. Every page reads its settings from `js/cnc-config.js`, every internal address is relative, and every API handler runs unchanged on Vercel or under `server/serve.js`. Moving into MCO is a configuration change, not a rewrite.

2.7 Nothing invented. Unknown values (the Google Tag Manager ID, the MCO portal address, MCO endpoints, the Grok model name) are null or pending, never guessed. What is known of the xAI API comes from search engine extracts of its documentation, not from a direct read (KERNEL-API.md section 7).

3.3 The commercial spine (Care Net).
3.3.1 Company account: `msp_client_account`, joined to the signed in person through `auth_user_id`. Accounts are created from a typed email without a sign in, so they start with no auth user. On every request, after the token check, `requireUser` calls `hsf_link_account` (contract 9.1, service role only): it returns the account already linked to the person, or else links the latest account that is not declined, has no auth user yet and whose contact email equals the person's confirmed Supabase email, and audits `client_auth_linked`. An unconfirmed email links nothing, and nobody else's account is ever taken over.
3.3.2 Medical Surveillance Plans: the existing MSP FORGE journey (assessment, OMP review, release). The portal shows the account's Plans from `hsf_portal_summary` (contract 9.8): the engagements whose intake used an assessment access token of the account, with reference, status, industry, revision and date.
3.3.3 Health and Safety Files: `hsf_file` and `hsf_file_item`, the compliance figure (SPEC B9.4), the gap report (SPEC B9.5), and the review and release gate (SPEC B11). By the Director's instruction of 23/09/2026 OMPs do not sign Files: a competent health and safety practitioner signs the safety content, under the rule in `hsf/SIGNOFF-CRITERIA.md`, which migration 053 builds (contract 10.8). Release needs an approved safety content sign off whose signatory's body and category fit the File's industry, with a registration current on the decision day, a register check and both an appointment letter and an engagement letter on record, and the client's section 16(2) acceptance; the OMP signed plan is Section E evidence and the chief executive acknowledgement is recorded, not a gate. Since 054 the register check must be no more than `hsf.signoff_register_check_max_days` (30) days old on the decision day (contract 11.4). One function, `hsf_release_rules`, states every rule: the release gate raises its first unmet reason, and `hsf_release_readiness` lists them all for the staff console without recording anything. The builder's setup form reads the industries, `hsf_public_subindustry` and `hsf_public_trigger` (contract 9.7, public read). A File or File item of another company answers exactly as one that does not exist, with 404 (contract 9.2). An account may generate at most `hsf.files_per_account_per_day` Files a day (20), and File references never truncate (contract 9.6). The portal shows each File's reference, status, revision, compliance figure and the sign offs of the current revision from the same summary.
3.3.4 Quotations: the existing quotation tables of Part A. The portal shows the quotations whose contact email is the account's (reference, package, price and its status, valid until, date), again from `hsf_portal_summary`.
3.3.5 `GET /api/portal-summary` is read only: unlike `/api/company-lookup`, which the portal no longer calls, it never approves an account or mints an assessment token. The endpoint rebuilds the reply from the named fields only, so nothing else the database might add reaches the browser.

3.4 The medical spine (MCO).
3.4.1 Certificates of fitness, training records and bookings, read from MCO through the adapter interface of SPEC B10 once HSF-3 closes. People are matched on MCO's own person reference, never on name (SPEC B10.3).
3.4.2 The documents companies drop into their File end up here, in MCO's secure environment for special personal information, after the transfer in section 4.
3.4.3 Until HSF-3 closes the portal shows each medical panel as "Arrives when MyClinicOnline is connected" and invents nothing. A clearly labelled demonstration uses the sample fixture only when the address carries `?demo=1`.

3.5 Staff. Forge roles in the token's `app_metadata.msp_roles` (forge_admin, forge_omp, forge_safety_reviewer and the others) show links to `review.html` and `settings.html`; each staff page checks the role again, and the database checks it a third time. Signed in staff can also read the view `hsf_staging_alerts` (section 4.2.10), the view `hsf_signatory_expiry_alerts` and the table `hsf_client_verification`, and can call `hsf_verify_client`, `hsf_revoke_client_verification` and `hsf_scan_reset`, which check the staff role in their body.
3.5.1 The staff console (contract 11.5) is `vercel/hsf-staff.html` on the shared shell, with `vercel/api/hsf-staff.js`. The API verifies the token with `requireUser` and then asks `hsf_user_is_staff`, which accepts forge_admin, forge_omp and forge_safety_reviewer; anyone else gets 403 before any console function runs. Every read and write goes through a service role function of 054 that takes the verified user, checks the staff role again in its body and records the staff member's email as `verified_by`, `revoked_by`, `created_by`, the `recorded_by` of an appended register check, or the audit actor. The staff email on a sign off sits only in its audit row, never in `hsf_signoff`, which the client reads.
3.5.2 Four tabs. Clients to verify: requested, verified and revoked accounts with company, contact email, request date, method and evidence reference; verify with a method and a required evidence reference, or revoke with a reason (`hsf_staff_verify_client`, `hsf_staff_revoke_client`). Scans and staging: uploads whose scan is pending or ended in an error, with attempts, the last findings and whether all five attempts are used, and the staging alerts with the date each document leaves staging; a stuck scan is given a fresh start with `hsf_staff_scan_reset`. File names are not shown. Signatories: every credential record with every 053 field, the latest register check, its age and the limit the gate applies, both letters and the expiry alerts (`hsf_staff_signatory_list`, `hsf_staff_signatory_save`, `hsf_staff_signatory_alerts`); credentials frozen by a release take only a recruitment portal reference, once, and fresh register checks as dated entries of their own. Sign offs and readiness: the Files with company, industry, revision, compliance figure, the latest decision of each kind and the release readiness, and a form that records a safety content sign off, the client's section 16(2) acceptance or the chief executive acknowledgement (`hsf_staff_signoff_record`, `hsf_staff_file_list`, `hsf_release_readiness`); an OMP sign off of a File is refused.
3.5.3 The page has labelled forms with plain errors, asks for revocation and scan reset confirmation inside the page (no browser dialogs), works with the keyboard and at 390 pixels wide, and carries `data-cta` on every action and an asset specification block like the other pages. With `?demo=1` it shows fictitious rows marked Sample and saves nothing. No other page links to it yet; staff open `/hsf-staff.html` directly (section 8.1, item 21).

3.6 Tracking and calls to action.
3.6.1 `cnc-tracking.js` sets Google consent mode to denied by default, shares the consent choice under the storage key `cnc_consent_v1`, and loads Google Tag Manager only when an ID is configured and the visitor has accepted. The ID is not known and stays null (pending), so no tag loads today.
3.6.2 Every call to action carries `data-cta`; a click pushes `{event: 'cnc_cta_click', cta, page}` and nothing personal. The four shared calls to action are "Build my File", "Build my Plan", "WhatsApp a sales executive" (https://wa.me/27600702723) and "Sign in" (`/portal.html`).

## 4. Document lifecycle

4.1 Before the first upload.
4.1.1 Uploads must be open. The parameter `hsf.uploads_open` (boolean, default false, group hsf) is set by a forge_admin through `msp_env_set`. It stays off until the backend (Odendaal) and the front end (Cassandra and the designer) are signed off. While it is off, `hsf_register_upload` refuses with "Document uploads open soon. Your File can be built now, and uploads will open once Care Net has finished testing." Consent, File generation and item status still work (contract 10.1).
4.1.2 The company contact signs in and has a company account that is not declined, linked on the first signed in request by `hsf_link_account` (section 3.3.1).
4.1.3 The company must be verified as a Care Net Consultants client (contract 10.2). `account_kind = 'approved_client'` is not enough, because migration 040 approves accounts itself. The builder asks for verification through `POST /api/hsf-upload {action: 'request_verification'}`, which calls `hsf_request_client_verification` and creates or returns the account's row in `hsf_client_verification` (status `requested`). A member of Care Net staff with a forge staff role then verifies the account in the staff console (`hsf_staff_verify_client`, section 3.5), or signed in forge staff call `hsf_verify_client` directly, with the method (`mco_company_ref`, `client_register` or `sales_executive`) and a required evidence reference, for example the MCO company reference or the client register number; `verified_by` records the staff email. `hsf_revoke_client_verification` (or `hsf_staff_revoke_client` in the console) revokes with a reason: nothing is deleted, and the account's untransferred uploads (`awaiting_upload`, `uploaded`, `verified`, `held`, `transferring`) get `transfer_blocked_reason = 'client verification revoked'`, so they are neither scanned nor claimed. A revoked account may ask again; a fresh verification does not lift blocks set by the revocation. Every step is audited. An unverified account is refused with "Uploads are for verified Care Net Consultants clients. Ask for verification in the builder, or WhatsApp a sales executive."
4.1.4 The contact gives three consents, each ticked separately and shown in full with the wording version `HSF-CONSENT-1.0`: document storage (in Care Net's private Supabase staging area, for the single purpose of building the File), MCO transfer (then deletion of the staging copy, with name, size, department and fingerprint kept as the audit trail), and authority to share (the contact is authorised by the employer and understands that health information is special personal information). Each is a row in `hsf_consent` with the time and the wording version; `hsf_consent_status` reports `complete` only when all three latest rows are granted and not withdrawn.
4.1.5 The builder reads `GET /api/hsf-upload?gate=1` (`hsf_upload_gate`), which answers `{uploads_open, client_verified, verification_requested, consent_complete, deletion_sms_available}`, and shows the matching message before any drop zone accepts a file. The gate only tells the builder what to show: `hsf_register_upload` enforces every rule itself.
4.1.6 The File skeleton exists (`hsf_generate_file`). Generating it needs no consent and no verification, because it holds no documents. It is refused for a declined account and above `hsf.files_per_account_per_day` Files a day.

4.2 The life of one document, step by step.
4.2.1 Choose. The contact drops a file on a section card or an element row and chooses the department (Executive and legal, Human resources, Health and safety, Operations, Engineering and maintenance, Procurement and contractors, Occupational health and medical, Training and development, Facilities and security). The browser rejects a wrong type or a file over 25 MB with a plain message before anything is sent; the server checks again against `hsf.upload_allowed_mime` and `hsf.upload_max_bytes`.
4.2.2 Fingerprint in the browser. The browser works out the file's SHA 256 with `crypto.subtle` before the file leaves the device. The queue shows it.
4.2.3 Register. `POST /api/hsf-upload {action: 'register', ...}` verifies the user's token, then `hsf_register_upload` refuses, in this order: no company account, a declined account, uploads closed (4.1.1), an account not verified (4.1.3), incomplete consent, then the department, section, File and element, and the type and size. It builds a safe file name and the staging path `<client_account_id>/<upload_id>/<safe_file_name>`, records the client fingerprint, sets status `awaiting_upload` and scan status `pending`, and audits.
4.2.4 Signed upload address. The server asks Supabase Storage for a one off signed upload address for that path and returns it. The service role key never leaves the server; the address carries its own short lived token.
4.2.5 Upload. The browser sends the bytes straight to Supabase Storage with `PUT`. The bytes never pass through the Vercel functions. Screen label: Uploading.
4.2.6 Complete. `POST /api/hsf-upload {action: 'complete'}` checks that the object is in the bucket at the registered size, then `hsf_mark_uploaded` moves the upload to `uploaded`. For an element upload it locks the File item row first, so two uploads never take the same evidence version, creates the `hsf_evidence` row (next version, source `client_upload`, SHA 256 from the client), marks the File item `uploaded` and recomputes the File's compliance figure. If a consent was withdrawn between registration and completion, the upload is `rejected` instead. Screen label: Uploaded, with "Security scan pending".
4.2.7 Scan and clean. On each run, after the sweep, the worker scans up to ten uploads (section 4.6). An upload that passes has its hidden details removed, and the cleaned copy is written over the staged object at the same path; the bytes the company sent are not kept (section 4.11). Only an upload whose scan is `clean` with a cleaned fingerprint (`sha256_clean`) goes further. An infected or harmful upload, or one the cleaner refuses, is `rejected` and its evidence withdrawn; its bytes leave through the cleanup queue. Screen label: Passed security scan, with "Hidden details removed: <labels>".
4.2.8 Claim. The transfer worker (the edge function `hsf-mco-transfer`, run on a schedule or by hand, service role only) reads the mode with `hsf_transfer_mode()` (hold when unset) and claims up to ten uploads, oldest first, with `hsf_transfer_claim` (contracts 9.5 and 10.4). The claim returns only uploads whose scan is `clean` and whose cleaned fingerprint is recorded (contract 11.1). In hold mode it returns only uploads in status `uploaded`, so a held document is not recorded again on every run. In fixture or live mode it returns `uploaded` and `held` uploads and any `transferring` upload claimed more than 30 minutes ago (a run that stopped part way), locks them so that two runs never take the same row, and marks them `transferring`. A blocked upload (consent withdrawn or verification revoked), or one of an account without current storage and transfer consent, is never claimed. The worker checks the scan status and the cleaned fingerprint again before it reads the bytes, and `hsf_transfer_record` refuses to record a held or received outcome for an upload that has not passed the scan with a cleaned copy.
4.2.9 Fingerprint on the server. The worker downloads each claimed object and works out the server SHA 256. Since 054 the staged bytes are the cleaned copy, so the server fingerprint is compared with the cleaned fingerprint `sha256_clean`; the client fingerprint stays on record as the proof of what the company sent. If they differ, the worker records `hash_mismatch` and sends nothing: the upload goes to `failed` with a reason, and `hsf_revoke_upload_evidence` revokes the evidence row written at completion, returns the File item to `outstanding` when no other unrevoked evidence holds it, and recomputes the compliance figure. Screen label: Not transferred: please upload it again.
4.2.10 Hold (today). In hold mode the adapter sends nothing. The worker records the outcome `held` and the upload stays in staging with status `held`. Screen label: Held for MyClinicOnline (the builder shows `transferring` the same way), with "Kept in Care Net staging until <date>" (section 4.7). The service role function `hsf_staging_alerts_list()` and the staff view `hsf_staging_alerts` list uploads holding bytes in staging for longer than `hsf.staging_alert_days` (14), uploads within 30 days of the two year limit (column `expires_on`), and uploads whose scan used all five attempts (column `scan_exhausted`). The staff console shows the list (section 3.5); no scheduled notice sends it yet (section 8.1, item 6).
4.2.11 Transfer (after MCO delivers the protocol). In live mode the adapter sends the file, its fingerprint and its filing details to MCO, and MCO answers with its document reference and the fingerprint of what it received. `hsf_transfer_record` (which accepts `uploaded`, `held` and `transferring` uploads) appends a row to `hsf_mco_transfer` (append only) and, when the outcome is `received` and the server and receipt fingerprints both equal the cleaned fingerprint, moves the upload to `transferred` with the MCO reference and time, and writes the MCO reference to the matching evidence row. Screen label: Transferred to MyClinicOnline.
4.2.12 Receipt check fails, or an error. A receipt fingerprint that differs is recorded as a mismatch, with the same effects as in 4.2.9. An error returns the upload to `uploaded`, to be claimed again on a later run. Neither deletes anything at that step.
4.2.13 Staging deletion after transfer. Only for an upload the database has just recorded as `transferred`, with the server and receipt fingerprints both equal to the cleaned fingerprint, the worker deletes the object through the Storage API and, after Storage confirms, calls `hsf_mark_staging_deleted`, which sets status `staging_deleted`, clears `storage_path` and records `staging_deleted_at`. Screen label: Removed from Care Net staging.
4.2.14 Sweep, cleanup and the two year limit, on every run. The order of a run is: mode, sweep, scan pass, transfer claim, cleanup, retention. `hsf_sweep_stale_uploads(24)` turns registrations still `awaiting_upload` after 24 hours into `failed` with the reason "The upload was not completed.", and returns a blocked upload whose transfer was claimed more than 30 minutes ago (a run that stopped part way; a blocked upload is never claimed again) to `uploaded`, as a transfer error would, so the company may delete it again (contract 11.2). Then `hsf_transfer_cleanup_queue` lists up to 25 uploads that still hold bytes and must leave staging, by the rule in `hsf_upload_awaits_cleanup`:
4.2.14.1 `transferred` (a deletion that did not finish) and `client_deleted` uploads, whether blocked or not;
4.2.14.2 `failed` and `rejected` uploads that are not blocked, that never completed, or that the scan found infected or harmful, whether blocked or not.
For each, the worker checks the path belongs to the upload, deletes the object, and only then calls `hsf_mark_staging_deleted`: a transferred upload becomes `staging_deleted`; a failed, rejected, client deleted or expired upload keeps its status and its path is cleared. Last, `hsf_retention_queue` lists up to 25 uploads past the two year limit and the worker deletes them (section 4.7). Both queues, and the marking functions, leave alone an upload a scan pass still holds (section 4.11.4), so a cleaned copy written back late is never left behind. A blocked upload that is still the client's document (it completed and did not fail the scan) is never cleaned up automatically: its bytes wait for the client's deletion (section 4.8) or the two year limit.
4.2.15 What remains with Care Net. The `hsf_upload` row (original and safe name, type, size, department, section, the client, server and cleaned fingerprints, the cleaned size and the list of hidden details removed (never their values), statuses and times, scan status, engine and findings, MCO reference, any block reason), the `hsf_mco_transfer` rows, the `hsf_evidence` row (append only; storage path cleared, MCO reference set, or revoked after a mismatch, a failed scan, the two year limit or a client deletion), the `hsf_deletion_request` rows and the `msp_audit` events. Never the bytes, a PIN or a key.

4.3 States and screen labels.

| Number | `hsf_upload.status` | Meaning | Screen label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `awaiting_upload` | Registered; bytes not yet confirmed in staging. After 24 hours the sweep makes it `failed` | Uploading |
| 2 | `uploaded` | Bytes in staging at the registered size; waiting for the scan and the removal of hidden details, then to be claimed | Uploaded |
| 3 | `verified` | Reserved in the contract's status list; not set by any step above | Uploaded |
| 4 | `held` | Passed the scan and cleaned; worker ran in hold mode; the cleaned copy still in staging | Held for MyClinicOnline |
| 5 | `transferring` | Claimed by a run in fixture or live mode; claimed again if the run stops for more than 30 minutes (a blocked one returns to `uploaded` through the sweep); never deletable by the company | Held for MyClinicOnline |
| 6 | `transferred` | MCO holds an identical copy; staging copy about to be deleted | Transferred to MyClinicOnline |
| 7 | `staging_deleted` | Staging copy deleted; row and fingerprints kept | Removed from Care Net staging |
| 8 | `rejected` | Refused, for example because consent was withdrawn before completion, the security scan failed or the cleaner could not remove the hidden details (reason recorded); bytes removed by the cleanup pass | Not accepted, or Failed security scan: <reason> |
| 9 | `failed` | Fingerprint mismatch, or never completed; evidence revoked; bytes removed by the cleanup pass; needs a person | Not transferred: please upload it again |
| 10 | `expired` | Reached the two year limit; bytes deleted, evidence withdrawn; row and fingerprints kept | Removed from Care Net staging at the two year limit |
| 11 | `client_deleted` | Deleted by the company with a PIN; evidence withdrawn; bytes removed by the cleanup pass; row and fingerprints kept | Deleted by your company |

4.3.1 `scan_status` sits beside the status: `pending` (Security scan pending), `clean` (Passed security scan), `infected` or `harmful` (Failed security scan: <reason>, and the upload is `rejected`), and `error` (still shown as pending; retried). `hsf_my_uploads` returns the scan status and time, `metadata_removed` (the plain labels of the hidden details removed, for "Hidden details removed: author, company, location"), `deletable`, `expires_on` and `transfer_blocked_reason` for the builder.
4.3.2 `transfer_blocked_reason` is not a status. It is set to `consent withdrawn` on an upload that was `uploaded`, `verified`, `held` or `transferring` when the account withdrew its storage or transfer consent (section 4.5), and to `client verification revoked` when Care Net revoked the account's verification (section 4.1.3). The builder shows the reason on the upload and offers the deletion journey for it (contract 10.6).

4.4 Rules the lifecycle never breaks.
4.4.1 The transfer step deletes nothing on `held`, on an error or on a mismatch. Every other deletion comes only from a database queue: the cleanup queue or the retention queue.
4.4.2 The staging copy of a transferred document is deleted only after MCO has confirmed an identical copy and the database has recorded the transfer.
4.4.3 `hsf_evidence` and `hsf_mco_transfer` are append only. The evidence trigger allows only the one way updates the contract names (revocation once; MCO reference and transfer time once; staging deletion once together with clearing the path). A replacement document is a new version.
4.4.4 The audit keeps fingerprints and the MCO reference, never file contents, a PIN, a key or a token.
4.4.5 Fixture mode, which pretends MCO received a file, and the fixture antivirus engine, which answers clean, are for tests only. Each is refused unless the function environment explicitly allows it (`HSF_MCO_ALLOW_FIXTURE=true`, `HSF_SCAN_ALLOW_FIXTURE=1`); neither may ever be allowed on the production project.
4.4.6 Nothing of an account that has withdrawn its storage or transfer consent, or whose verification was revoked, is moved automatically, and nothing that may still be its document is deleted automatically before the two year limit. Only the company, with a PIN, deletes it sooner (sections 4.5 and 4.8).
4.4.7 The worker never deletes a path the database did not list for that upload, and marks a deletion or an expiry only after Storage confirms the object is gone.
4.4.8 Nothing is held or sent to MCO before it has passed the security scan and its hidden details have been removed; what moves is the cleaned copy, checked against `sha256_clean`.
4.4.9 A document on its way to MCO (status `transferring`) is never deletable by the company, whatever the age of the claim, because MCO may already hold that copy (contract 11.2). If the transfer errors, or a blocked transfer that stopped part way is returned by the sweep, the upload is `uploaded` again and deletable again. The two year limit does not take a transfer claimed in the last 30 minutes.
4.4.10 The bytes the company sent are not kept once the cleaned copy is written; a document the cleaner cannot clean is rejected, never passed as it is.
4.4.11 The scan pass never writes a cleaned copy back for an upload that has left the scan (for example one the company deleted), so a deleted document is never put back in staging.

4.5 Withdrawal of consent (contracts 9.5 and 10.6).
4.5.1 Any withdrawal stops new uploads at once: registration needs all three consents, and an upload not yet completed is `rejected` on completion.
4.5.2 Withdrawing the document storage or the MCO transfer consent also sets `transfer_blocked_reason = 'consent withdrawn'` on every upload of the account that is `uploaded`, `verified`, `held` or `transferring`, and counts them in the audit event. A blocked upload is never scanned or claimed for transfer and, while it may still be the company's document, is left out of the cleanup queue, so its bytes stay in staging, untouched, and appear in the staging alerts once they pass `hsf.staging_alert_days`.
4.5.3 Nothing is deleted automatically before the two year limit. The Director decided on 23/09/2026 that the builder shows each blocked upload with its reason and offers the company the deletion journey of section 4.8, and that the two year limit of section 4.7 still applies to blocked uploads.
4.5.4 A later consent does not lift the block; a person must decide for each blocked upload.
4.5.5 Withdrawing only the authority to share consent stops new uploads but does not block documents already in staging from moving. Whether it should is still open (section 8.1, item 5).
4.5.6 Withdrawal does not reach documents already transferred to MCO; what happens to them is set under the MCO contract (HSF-3, section 5.8.3).

4.6 The security scan (contracts 10.4 and 11.1). The Director decided on 23/09/2026 that every upload is always scanned for viruses, malware and all metadata; the removal of the metadata is section 4.11.
4.6.1 Claim. `hsf_scan_claim(10)` returns uploads in status `uploaded` with scan status `pending` or `error`, not blocked, with bytes present and fewer than five attempts, oldest first, skipping rows another worker has locked, and counts one attempt on each. Since 054 an upload another pass holds (claimed, or its write back announced, in the last 30 minutes) is not handed out, and each claimed upload gets a new claim identifier. The scan pass runs in every mode, hold included.
4.6.2 Fingerprint. The worker downloads the object and checks that its SHA 256 equals the browser fingerprint and, when one is recorded, the server fingerprint, or else the fingerprint of a cleaned copy an earlier pass announced (section 4.11.4). A mismatch records `error` with the finding `fingerprint_mismatch`: the upload fails like a mismatch at transfer, with its evidence withdrawn, and is not retried.
4.6.3 Structural check. `inspectBytes` in `scan-core.js` always runs and flags as harmful: content whose first bytes do not match the declared type; PDF active content (JavaScript, launch actions, open actions with JavaScript, embedded files, rich media, XFA forms, automatic actions), including names hidden by escapes or inside compressed object streams; OOXML macros (`vbaProject.bin`), ActiveX, embedded OLE objects and relationships that load content from outside; legacy Office macros and embedded objects; CSV cells that start like a formula; JPEG and PNG images carrying more than 256 KB of metadata. In the scan pass the GPS location details of an image no longer reject it: the cleaner removes them (section 4.11), and the cleaned copy is checked again and rejected if a location is still there. Anything that cannot be inspected (a truncated structure, an encrypted or zip64 archive, a compression the runtime cannot read) is `error`, never `clean`.
4.6.4 Antivirus engine. `createAvEngine` posts the raw bytes to `HSF_AV_ENDPOINT` with the bearer `HSF_AV_TOKEN`, `Content-Type: application/octet-stream` and the header `X-File-Name`, and expects `{clean, signature, engine}`. The engine is mandatory: without it every scan records `error` with the finding "Antivirus engine not configured" and nothing transfers.
4.6.5 Record. A clean result is recorded only by `hsf_scan_record_clean`, together with the cleaned copy (section 4.11); since 054 `hsf_scan_record` refuses `clean`. Every other result goes through `hsf_scan_record`, and recording any result ends the pass's hold. `infected` or `harmful` (including a file the cleaner refuses): the upload is `rejected` with "The file failed the security scan: <plain summary of the findings>", any block is cleared, the evidence is withdrawn, the File item returns to outstanding when nothing else holds it, and the bytes leave through the cleanup queue; audited as `hsf_upload_scan_rejected`. `error`: the upload stays `uploaded` for a retry. An error that only says the antivirus engine is not configured or did not answer gives its attempt back, so an outage never uses up the five attempts; only inspection errors count.
4.6.6 Stuck scans. An upload whose scan used all five attempts is not claimed again and appears in the staging alerts with `scan_exhausted`. Staff give it a fresh start with `hsf_scan_reset(upload_id)`, which accepts only an `uploaded` document with scan status `pending` or `error` and bytes present, sets the attempts to 0 and audits `hsf_upload_scan_reset`. It may be called by the service role or by signed in forge staff; the staff console calls it through `hsf_staff_scan_reset`, which records the staff member's email (section 3.5).
4.6.7 One document is held in memory at a time. Findings carry plain words for the client and never the file name, the content or a key.

4.7 The two year limit (contract 10.3). The Director decided on 23/09/2026 that a document stays in the private bucket for at most two years.
4.7.1 The parameter `hsf.staging_retention_days` (default 730, held between 1 and 730 whatever the stored value) sets the limit, counted from `uploaded_at`, or `created_at` for an upload that never completed.
4.7.2 `hsf_retention_queue` lists, oldest first, uploads holding bytes past the limit, blocked ones included: the two year limit overrides a block. Transferred and client deleted uploads are left out because they already leave through the cleanup queue, and so is a transfer claimed in the last 30 minutes, and an upload a scan pass still holds (section 4.11.4).
4.7.3 The worker deletes each object through the Storage API and then calls `hsf_mark_expired`, which sets status `expired`, clears the path, records `staging_deleted_at` on the upload and its evidence, withdraws the evidence (the File item returns to outstanding when nothing else holds it, and the compliance figure is recomputed) and audits `hsf_upload_expired`.
4.7.4 The builder shows "Kept in Care Net staging until <date>" for each staged upload the company can still delete, from `expires_on` in `hsf_my_uploads`. Uploads the cleanup queue removes on its next run carry no date.

4.8 Deletion by the company, with a warning and a PIN (contract 10.5). The Director decided on 23/09/2026 that a company may delete its own documents after a warning and a PIN sent by email or SMS.
4.8.1 What may be deleted. The company's own uploads whose bytes are still in Care Net staging: any status except `transferring`, `transferred`, `staging_deleted`, `expired` and `client_deleted`. A document on its way to MCO is never deletable, whatever the age of the claim; it becomes deletable again only if the transfer stops and it returns to `uploaded` (section 4.4.9, contract 11.2). The builder never offers a document in transfer for deletion and says why. Once MCO holds a document, deletion is MCO's process and the builder says so. `hsf_my_uploads` returns `deletable` for each upload.
4.8.2 The journey. The contact selects uploads (at most 50), reads the warning "Deleting is permanent. Care Net cannot recover a deleted document, and any File item it supported will return to outstanding.", ticks "I understand this cannot be undone", chooses email or SMS, receives a one time PIN, enters it and confirms.
4.8.3 Request. `POST /api/hsf-delete {action: 'request', upload_ids, channel, acknowledged: true}` calls `hsf_deletion_request_create`, which checks ownership and that every upload is deletable (a document of another account reads as one that cannot be deleted), allows at most 5 requests per account per hour (SQLSTATE `PT429`, answered as 429), cancels the account's earlier open requests, and returns `{request_id, expires_at, channel, destination_hint}` (for example f***@example.co.za, or the last four digits of the phone). The request expires 10 minutes after it is made.
4.8.4 The PIN is sent and checked by Supabase Auth; Care Net never generates or stores a PIN. The server calls `POST <SUPABASE_URL>/auth/v1/otp` with the person's email (or confirmed phone) and `create_user: false`, then records `hsf_deletion_request_pin_sent`. If Supabase Auth cannot send the PIN, the request is cancelled with `hsf_deletion_request_cancel`, so a request whose own PIN never went out can never be confirmed.
4.8.5 Confirm. `POST /api/hsf-delete {action: 'confirm', request_id, pin}` takes a PIN of 6 to 10 digits. `hsf_deletion_request_attempt` counts the attempt before the check: after 5 attempts on one request the next locks it, after 10 minutes it reads expired, and an account with 5 attempts in the last hour, counted over every request open at any time in that hour, is refused whatever the request (contract 11.3). The server then calls `POST <SUPABASE_URL>/auth/v1/verify` with `{type: 'email' | 'sms', email | phone, token}`; a 200 whose user id equals the signed in user is a pass, and the session Supabase Auth opens is signed out again at once. A wrong PIN answers the same whether it was close or not. Rate limit refusals (the database, Supabase Auth, a locked request) are 429.
4.8.6 Deletion. `hsf_deletion_request_confirm` accepts only the caller's pending, unexpired request whose PIN was sent and attempted, checks again that every upload is still deletable (all or nothing), sets each upload `client_deleted` with any block cleared, withdraws its evidence, recomputes the compliance figure, and audits `hsf_upload_client_deleted` with the fingerprints, never the bytes or the file name. The bytes leave on the next run through the cleanup queue (section 4.2.14).
4.8.7 SMS. The builder offers SMS only when `hsf.deletion_sms_enabled` is true and the sign in has a confirmed phone (`deletion_sms_available` in the gate). Both need an SMS provider configured in Supabase Auth (section 8.2, item 3).

4.9 A receipt from MCO after the company deleted the document. If a transfer was in flight when the company deleted the document or the two year limit took it, and MCO then confirms receipt with server and receipt fingerprints matching the cleaned fingerprint, `hsf_transfer_record` keeps the MCO reference and the receipt, appends an `hsf_mco_transfer` row that asks for the deletion at MCO, and audits `hsf_transfer_received_after_deletion` with the action "Ask MyClinicOnline to delete this document." The status stays `client_deleted` or `expired`, and the cleanup queue removes any bytes still staged. Because a document in transfer is never deletable by the company and the two year limit waits 30 minutes after a claim (section 4.4.9), this arises only when the two year limit takes a transfer claimed more than 30 minutes earlier, or when a transfer stopped, the document returned to `uploaded` and was deleted, and MCO's receipt of the earlier attempt then arrives. No notice sends the audit event to a person yet (section 8.1, item 6).

4.10 The transfer protocol belongs to MCO (contract 10.7). The Director decided on 23/09/2026 that MCO builds the transfer protocol and that documents stay in Supabase staging until then. `hsf/MCO-TRANSFER-REQUIREMENTS.md` sets out, as Care Net's proposal awaiting MCO's design, what the worker sends and expects: the adapter's send contract, the receipt with SHA 256, idempotency on the upload id, person and company matching, error codes, deletion at MCO, audit, signed requests or mutual TLS, handling of special personal information, and retention.

4.11 Removal of hidden details before a document leaves staging (contract 11.1). The Director's rule of 23/09/2026: scan for viruses, malware and all metadata.
4.11.1 After the structural check and the antivirus engine have passed, the scan pass asks `cleanMetadata` in `supabase/functions/_shared/metadata-clean.js` for a copy with every non essential hidden detail removed, and writes it over the staged object at the same path (Storage upload with `x-upsert: true`). The bytes the company sent are not kept.
4.11.2 What is removed. JPEG: every APPn segment except the JFIF header and the ICC colour profile (so EXIF, including location, XMP, IPTC and thumbnails go) and every comment segment; the Adobe segment a CMYK picture needs for its colours stays. PNG: text, compressed text, international text, EXIF and time chunks and every other ancillary chunk outside the kept list. Word and Excel (OOXML): custom properties, thumbnails, comments and the people who commented, with their relationships and content type entries; the core properties become a minimal part dated the upload day, without creator, last editor, title, subject, keywords, description, category or revision; company, manager, hyperlink base and template leave the extended properties; pictures inside the document are cleaned as pictures. PDF: every value of the document information dictionary and every XMP metadata stream is blanked in place at the same byte length, so every cross reference offset stays valid. CSV: only a UTF 8 byte order mark.
4.11.3 What is refused, with a plain reason, so the upload is rejected like a failed scan and its bytes leave through the cleanup queue: an older Word or Excel file ("Older Word and Excel files cannot have their hidden details removed. Please save it as a .docx or .xlsx file and upload it again."); a PDF that keeps its details inside a compressed object stream, is encrypted, or names an object the cleaner could not follow ("This PDF keeps its details in a form Care Net cannot clean. Please print it to a new PDF or save it again, then upload it."); and anything else the cleaner cannot parse. Nothing is ever passed as it is.
4.11.4 Writing back safely. The write back is the one scan step that changes staging. Before it, `hsf_scan_write_back` records the cleaned fingerprint, size and list of removed details while the upload is still held by the calling pass (its claim identifier, a 30 minute hold the announcement renews). It is refused once the upload has left the scan, for example because the company deleted it, and then nothing is written. While a pass holds an upload, no other pass claims it and the cleanup and retention queues leave its object alone. If a pass stops after the write, the next one accepts the announced cleaned copy as the upload's own bytes and scans it again rather than reading it as a fingerprint mismatch.
4.11.5 The cleaned fingerprint. `hsf_scan_record_clean` records scan `clean` together with `sha256_clean`, `size_bytes_clean`, `cleaned_at` and `metadata_removed` (at most 50 plain labels, never the removed values), and only with the announced fingerprint. Only such an upload is claimed for transfer; the transfer and the staging deletion compare the server and receipt fingerprints with `sha256_clean` (sections 4.2.9 and 4.2.13). The client fingerprint stays on record as the proof of what the company sent.
4.11.6 The builder shows "Hidden details removed: <labels>" once the cleaned copy is recorded, for example author, company, location.

## 5. POPIA analysis in plain words

5.1 Standing of this section. This is the build team's reading, written so the Information Officer and an attorney can check it quickly. It is not legal advice and it claims nothing about meeting POPIA. In the live kernel the Protection of Personal Information Act, 2013 (POPIA) has not passed the three checks. Migration 042, in the repository but not applied (HSF-7), records a documentary verification of it for the medical scope, subject to OMP ratification, and for the File it sits in verification batch HSF-VER-01. This section therefore describes its duties in words and cites no section numbers.

5.3 Special personal information. Health information about a person is special personal information under POPIA, and the Act prohibits processing it unless one of the authorisations the Act sets out applies, for example the person's consent or a specific authorisation for particular kinds of body or purpose. Two points follow:
5.3.1 The people the health information is about are the employees, not the company. The three consents are given by the company contact for the company. The third consent confirms the contact's authority to share and acknowledges that health information is special personal information, but it is not the employee's own consent.
5.3.2 Whether the employer's own authority to hold its employees' occupational health records, together with the Act's authorisations, covers sharing those records with Care Net and MCO for the File, or whether employee consent or another basis is needed, is a question for the attorney (section 5.10, item 1). Until it is answered, the safer course is to guide companies to upload outcomes and administrative records (appointments, certificates of fitness showing fit, fit with a restriction or not fit, training certificates) rather than clinical records, in line with SPEC Part A 8.2 and B10.4.

5.4 Purpose limitation.
5.4.1 One purpose: building and keeping the company's Health and Safety File, and moving its documents to MCO where they are kept. The consent wording says so.
5.4.2 Documents and their metadata are not used for marketing, not used to train any model, and not sent to xAI or any other AI provider.
5.4.3 The kernel API carries no client data at all (KERNEL-API.md section 3), so the Grok bot cannot reach client data through it.
5.4.4 Further use for another purpose would need its own lawful basis and, where consent is the basis, its own consent (as the default off marketing consent of Part A 8.1).

5.5 Who is responsible and who are the operators.
5.5.1 The employer is the responsible party for its employees' records. Whether Care Net acts as an operator for the employer when it stores and moves documents on the employer's instruction, or as a responsible party in its own right because it decides how the File platform works, affects the contracts needed and is for the attorney (section 5.10, item 2).
5.5.2 The operators that process personal information for Care Net in this design:

A search engine extract of xAI's security FAQ (not read directly) gives a default 30 day retention of API requests for abuse monitoring and zero data retention for enterprise accounts (KERNEL-API.md section 7.2, finding 7); to be confirmed from xAI's own pages |
| 5 | The antivirus engine at `HSF_AV_ENDPOINT` | The bytes of every upload, while it is scanned | Not chosen yet (section 8.2, item 1). 5.6 Security safeguards in the design.
5.6.1 Row level security on every table; anonymous and public access revoked; every write through security definer functions called only by the server after it has verified the user's token. A record of another company answers exactly as one that does not exist (404), so an identifier reveals nothing.
5.6.2 Staging is private with no storage policy for anonymous or signed in users; only the service role reads or deletes. Uploads use one off signed addresses with a short lived token. Everything travels over HTTPS.
5.6.3 Fingerprints taken in the browser, on the server and by MCO on receipt detect any change or corruption on the way. They show integrity, not confidentiality.
5.6.4 Append only audit (`msp_audit`), evidence and transfer tables.
5.6.5 No secret in any file or page; server secrets live only in the host's environment. The kernel API stores only the SHA 256 of each key.
5.6.6 Every upload is scanned before it can move: a built in structural check for active content, macros, outside links, formula injection and oversized metadata, and a mandatory antivirus engine (section 4.6). All non essential metadata, location details included, is then removed and only the cleaned copy is kept and moved (section 4.11), which also keeps names and places hidden in a file's properties from reaching MCO. Uploads are open only to companies Care Net has verified as clients (section 4.1.3), and a company's deletion needs a PIN that Supabase Auth sends and checks (section 4.8).
5.6.7 The staff console (section 3.5) is open only to forge staff, checked by the API and again in every database function, and records who did what. The staging alerts and the signatory expiry alerts are shown there; no scheduled notice sends them yet (section 8.1, item 6).

5.7 Transfers outside South Africa. POPIA limits sending personal information to a third party in another country unless conditions in the Act are met (for example laws or binding rules that give adequate protection, a contract, or the person's consent). The Part A privacy policy approach discloses cross border transfers (SPEC Part A 8.3). For this design:
5.7.1 The Supabase project region and the Vercel function region must be confirmed and recorded.
5.7.2 xAI is a United States company; where it processes API requests must be confirmed from its terms. Only question text and public kernel data should reach it.
5.7.3 MCO's hosting location is unknown until HSF-3. The antivirus engine's and the SMS provider's locations are unknown until they are chosen.
5.7.4 The privacy policy at https://www.carenetconsultants.co.za/privacy-policy must name each of these and the basis relied on, as the attorney advises.

5.8 Retention.
5.8.1 Staging: only until the transfer is confirmed, then deleted automatically. Bytes of failed, rejected, scan rejected, never completed and client deleted uploads are removed by the cleanup pass. While the worker runs in hold mode (until MCO delivers the transfer protocol) held documents stay in staging, and so do documents blocked by a consent withdrawal or a revoked verification, but never for longer than two years: the Director decided on 23/09/2026 that two years is the maximum, after which the bytes are deleted and the evidence withdrawn (section 4.7). The company may delete them sooner with a PIN (section 4.8).
5.8.2 Metadata, fingerprints and audit: kept with the File as its evidence trail. Retention per record class is open (HSF-5, CR-12.4). Occupational health records carry long statutory retention and never default to short cycles (SPEC Part A 8.4); the house floor for medical surveillance records of hazardous exposure is 40 years where an instrument sets none (SPEC B5.3, RULE-RETAIN), and the kernel pack plans 40 years for files triggered by the noise and physical agents regulations (kernel pack 05, section 4).
5.8.3 Documents in MCO: MCO's retention, set under the MCO contract.
5.8.4 The kernel API call log and the Grok bot host's own logs: no retention period is set yet (section 8.1, item 12).

5.9 Rights of the people the information is about.
5.9.1 Access, correction, deletion and objection requests from employees go first to the employer as responsible party; Care Net and MCO help as the contracts provide. The Part A engagement export, correction and deletion workflows (SPEC Part A 8.4) extend to File data.
5.9.2 Deletion meets retention: the staging copy is deleted automatically, and the company may delete its own staged documents with a PIN, but the audit trail (fingerprints, never bytes) and statutory records may have to be kept. How a deletion request is answered when a record must be kept needs a written rule (section 5.10, item 5). A document already at MCO is deleted through MCO's process.
5.9.3 Withdrawal of consent stops new uploads at once, and withdrawal of the storage or the transfer consent stops the company's staged documents from moving, without deleting them. The company may then delete them with a PIN, and the two year limit still applies (section 4.5).
5.9.4 The Information Officer must be named in the privacy notice and in every pack's POPIA block (CR-12.6, open).

## 6. Hosting options

6.1 Now: Vercel. The static pages and the `vercel/api` functions run on the Vercel project; Supabase provides the database, sign in, staging storage and the transfer worker. Server secrets (`SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`) live in the Vercel environment only.

6.2 Later: `server/serve.js` on MCO infrastructure. The dependency free Node server serves `vercel/` with the same clean addresses, redirects and headers as `vercel.json`, and mounts every `vercel/api/*.js` handler at `/api/<name>` with the request helpers the handlers expect. Run it with Node 18 or later behind MCO's reverse proxy, which terminates TLS: `node server/serve.js --port 3000` (or `PORT` and `HOST=0.0.0.0` in a container).

6.3 Comparison.

| Number | Concern | Vercel now | `server/serve.js` in MCO later |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Where pages and API run | Vercel | MCO servers or containers |
| 2 | Secrets | Vercel environment | MCO's secret store, injected as environment variables |
| 3 | TLS and domain | Vercel | MCO reverse proxy; domain pending |
| 4 | Scaling | Automatic | Run more than one instance behind the proxy; the server keeps no state of its own |
| 5 | Logs | Vercel logs | One line per request (time, method, path without the query, status, duration) |
| 6 | Database, sign in, staging | Supabase | Supabase (unchanged) until a later decision moves any of it |
| 7 | Transfer worker | Supabase edge function | Stays in Supabase, or runs beside the server: the adapter and core are plain ES modules |

6.4 What changes on the move.
6.4.1 `js/cnc-config.js` (or `window.CNC_CONFIG_OVERRIDES` set by the MCO shell): `authProvider`, `mcoPortalUrl` and, if the API sits on another origin, `apiBase`.
6.4.2 The Supabase Auth site address and redirect allow list gain the MCO addresses, so sign in links return to the right place.
6.4.3 Browser storage is kept per origin, so a visitor's cookie choice and any stored session on the Vercel address do not carry to the MCO address; visitors choose again once.
6.4.4 The kernel API base address given to the Grok bot (`CNC_KERNEL_API_BASE`) changes, and the bot host's secret is updated.
6.4.5 DNS, and the redirect from the old addresses, are set when the MCO domain is known.

## 7. Sign in abstraction

7.1 Now: Supabase Auth. `js/cnc-auth.js` offers `init`, `signInWithEmail`, `session`, `accessToken`, `onChange` and `signOut`. With the provider `supabase` it signs people in with an email one time link, exactly as the landing page does, so one session serves every page. Each API call carries the access token; `vercel/lib/auth.js` asks Supabase Auth whose token it is, calls `hsf_link_account` once (section 3.3.1) and passes only the verified user id to the database, which finds the company through `msp_client_account.auth_user_id`. If Supabase Auth or the database cannot be reached the API answers 503 rather than 401, so the pages can tell an outage from a lapsed sign in and do not show "no company account" because of an outage.

7.2 Later: MCO single sign on (pending HSF-3). The provider `mco_sso` exists as a stub that refuses with "MCO single sign on is pending the MCO interface contract (HSF-3)". Nothing is guessed about how MCO signs people in. When the contract arrives, one of three shapes will be chosen:
7.2.1 MCO as an identity provider federated into Supabase Auth, if MCO supports a standard protocol Supabase accepts. The database keeps its user ids and nothing below the sign in changes. Preferred where possible.
7.2.2 The web tier validates MCO's own tokens (a second branch in `requireUser`) and maps each MCO user to a Care Net user id and company account through a mapping table.
7.2.3 A token exchange, in which a verified MCO sign in is exchanged for a Care Net session.

7.3 Whatever the shape, staff roles stay Care Net's: the forge roles in `app_metadata.msp_roles` are granted by Care Net, never inferred from an MCO claim unless the Director agrees otherwise, and the database keeps checking them.

7.4 The medical spine's data reaches the portal only through the server side adapter (SPEC B10), with the employer's view limited to outcome, restriction, dates and practitioner identity. The browser never calls MCO with a Care Net secret.
