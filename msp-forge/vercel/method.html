Why your Plan holds up when it matters

A Medical Surveillance Plan is only as strong as the law behind it. Most plans are written once, copied often, and quietly go stale while the regulations move on. The Care Net Method is our answer: a maintained, versioned body of South African occupational health law that every Plan is built against, reviewed monthly, and ratified by a registered Occupational Medical Practitioner.

Three commitments sit underneath every document we release:

  • Triple verification. No regulation is cited until it passes three independent checks: the primary legal text itself, a second authoritative source that corroborates it, and a currency check that confirms it is still in force and captures every amendment. A law that cannot pass all three gates never appears in your Plan.
  • A legal basis for every test. Every medical assessment we prescribe carries two justifications: the workplace hazard that demands it, and section 7 of the Employment Equity Act that makes testing lawful against the inherent requirements of the job. No test without a reason; no reason without a law.
  • Clinical sign off, enforced. No Plan is released without review, recommendation, and signature by a registered Occupational Medical Practitioner. That gate lives in our systems themselves, not in a policy document, so there is no way around it.

The framework carries a version number on every release, an audit trail on every change, and a monthly review cycle that tracks the law as it moves: the 2026 transition from the Noise Induced Hearing Loss Regulations to the Noise Exposure Regulations, for example, is already built in and swaps automatically on the effective date.

316 job roles, each with its own risk picture

A cashier and a forklift driver in the same building need entirely different surveillance. That is why the Method maps job roles, not just industries: 316 roles across the taxonomy, each carrying its inherent physical and cognitive demands, its statutory competency requirements, and its typical hazard exposures with a rating and a reason.

When you complete the assessment, your own job categories are matched against this library, then adjusted to your actual operations: your measured exposure levels, your controls, your shift patterns. Where your measurements exceed a legal limit, the surveillance tightens. It never loosens by assumption.

Examples of what a role carries: a rock drill operator maps noise, silica dust, vibration, and underground heat, and routes dust disease surveillance through the statutory mines pathway; a contact centre agent maps night shift patterns, workstation strain, and headset acoustic exposure. Same country, same law, completely different Plans.
