Why this matters

A plan on the shelf is not a plan that protects you.

Most Medical Surveillance Plans are written once, copied between sites, and quietly go out of date while the regulations move on. The plan still looks fine in the file. It stops holding up the moment somebody tests it.

  • An inspector asks which regulation requires a test, and the plan cannot say.
  • A client audit finds medicals being done that no hazard on site calls for.
  • A worker leaves and no exit medical was ever planned for the job.
  • A law is replaced, and nobody notices until the next incident.

We built our Plans to survive all four of those moments.

The Care Net Method

Three promises behind every page we release.

Your Plan is built against a maintained body of South African occupational health law that carries a version number, an audit trail on every change, and a review every month.

01

Every law checked three ways

No regulation reaches your Plan until it passes three separate checks: the primary legal text, a second authoritative source that agrees with it, and a currency check that confirms it is still in force today. Anything that fails a check is left out rather than guessed at.

02

A legal reason for every test

Each medical assessment carries two reasons: the hazard on your site that calls for it, and section 7 of the Employment Equity Act, which is what makes testing a worker lawful in the first place. No test without a reason. No reason without a law.

03

A practitioner signs, or it does not go out

A registered Occupational Medical Practitioner reviews the draft, records recommendations, and signs with their HPCSA number. That gate sits inside our systems, not in a policy document, so no Plan can be released around it.

One example of the review at work: the Noise Induced Hearing Loss Regulations are replaced by the Noise Exposure Regulations on 6 September 2026. That change is already loaded, with the new action levels for workers exposed to both noise and certain chemicals, and it takes effect in your Plan on the day the law does.

What is inside your Plan

Eleven sections an auditor can follow line by line.

Your Plan is written to be read by the people who test it: your safety team, your client's auditor, and the inspector at the gate.

The legal frame

Every Act and regulation that governs your industry, with its gazette reference, and what each one requires of you. No generic list, only what applies to your operation.

The risk profile per job

Every job category with its duties, the hazards it carries, an exposure rating with the reason for it, and how your measured levels compare to the legal limits.

The surveillance programme

Per job: the baseline medical before placement, the periodic interval, the exit medical, and any biological monitoring, each tied to the law that calls for it.

The Plan also carries your inherent job requirements, your chemical register, respirator fit test status, responsibilities by role, the confidentiality and personal information sections required under POPIA, and the practitioner sign off page.

Why the signature is charged and the Plan is not.

Your Plan is drafted by a trained language model working from a framework we update every month for legislation and again for job roles and risk. That framework is strong. It is still not a person, and we will not put a practitioner's name on a document no person has read. The review is the practitioner's time, and it is priced on the number of people whose surveillance they are signing off.

All amounts exclude VAT and are confirmed on your quotation before work begins. Industries the framework does not yet hold go to a sales executive for a tailored quotation rather than an automatic figure.

Always protect yourself

Your records, kept for as long as the law requires.

Health legislation sets different retention periods for different records. Some are kept for 5 years, and medical surveillance records for hazardous exposure for 40 years. Paper records are easy to lose, hard to secure, and seldom survive those periods intact, which puts both your POPIA compliance and your audit defence at risk. Your Plan, and the medicals that run from it, sit in secure digital storage for as long as the law prescribes, ready for audits and access requests.

Questions we are asked

Straight answers, no small print.

What is a Medical Surveillance Plan?

It is a written document that sets out, for every job category in your workplace, which occupational medical assessments your employees need, how often they need them, and the law that requires each one. It is the plan your medicals run from, and the document an inspector, an auditor or a client asks to see.

Does my company need one?

If your risk assessment shows employees are exposed to a hazard that South African law places under medical surveillance, such as noise, dust, hazardous chemicals, biological agents or work at height, then a written programme is required. The Occupational Health and Safety Act and its regulations place that duty on the employer.

How long does it take?

The assessment takes about 30 to 45 minutes with your risk assessment at hand. Your draft is prepared from that assessment, then reviewed and signed by a registered Occupational Medical Practitioner before it is released to you.

Do you do the medicals as well?

Yes. Once your Plan is signed, a mobile clinic can come to your site for 10 or more workers, or your people can walk into any of our four fixed clinics. The Plan tells the clinic exactly what to do for each job.

What if we already have a plan?

Bring it. We will run the same assessment, show you where the two differ, and tell you plainly which of your current tests have a legal basis and which do not. That comparison is often the most useful part.

What happens when the law changes?

The framework behind your Plan is reviewed every month and carries a version number. When a regulation is amended or replaced, the change is verified against the primary source first, and your revision then follows the same clinical review as the original.

How is our information protected?

The assessment asks about jobs and hazards, never about named individuals or their health. It is designed that way on purpose. Everything is processed under POPIA for the single purpose of designing your programme, and you can withdraw consent at any time.

Who signs the Plan?

A registered Occupational Medical Practitioner reviews the draft, records clinical recommendations, and signs with their name and HPCSA registration number. No Plan is released without that signature.
