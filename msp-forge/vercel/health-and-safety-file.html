What is in the File

Fifteen sections. Every element evidenced or flagged.

Every element carries the duty in plain words, the law it rests on, who is responsible, the evidence required, how often it is reviewed and where it stands today: linked from MyClinicOnline, uploaded, outstanding, expired, or not applicable with a written reason. Nothing else.

Your industry adds its own elements on top: for example the full Construction Regulations for building and civils work, the Mine Health and Safety Act regime at a mine, or PSIRA and firearm competency for security services. A universal element is never removed silently; if it does not apply, the File says so and why.

      The law behind your File

      Nothing is cited until it has been checked three times.

      Every instrument in your File is verified against its primary text, corroborated by an independent source, and checked for currency on the day. An instrument that fails a check is left out rather than guessed at, and the element that depends on it shows as awaiting verification instead. The register is reviewed every month; when the law moves, your File opens a revision.

      What the File does, and what it does not.It assembles, evidences, flags and reports. It does not decide that a workplace is safe, certify that a person is competent or determine anyone's fitness for work. The accountable people decide and sign: the Health and Safety Manager signs the safety content, your section 16(2) appointee accepts the File and your chief executive acknowledges it. The Occupational Medical Practitioner signs the Medical Surveillance Plan, a separate Care Net product, which the File holds in Section E as evidence.
